# Land Use, Housing & Homelessness Data Inventory — Portland / Multnomah County / Oregon

## Overview

This inventory covers publicly available data sources for land use, housing production, property/tax lot records, zoning, regional land information, affordable housing, and homelessness for the City of Portland, Multnomah County, Metro (regional government), and the State of Oregon, plus federal (HUD) sources scoped to this geography. **38 distinct sources** are documented across five jurisdictional groupings. The strongest cluster is City of Portland permitting/zoning data and Multnomah County parcel/assessor data — both machine-readable, address/parcel-level, and continuously updated. The weakest cluster is homelessness and affordable-housing *production* data, which is dominated by PDF quarterly/annual reports rather than open datasets, with the notable exception of Multnomah County's new (April 2025) Homeless Services data dashboard and HUD's national PIT/HIC CSV exports.

A tax lot / property ID and street address are the dominant join keys tying together permits, zoning, assessed value, and affordable-housing inventory records across jurisdictions. Homelessness data (PIT counts, HDX, Joint Office/HSD dashboards) generally has no individual-record join key by design (privacy-protected, de-identified aggregates) and joins only at the CoC/county/month level.

## Standouts

**Best for maps:**
- **Multnomah County Taxlot Parcels (#14)** joined with **PortlandMaps assessor/permit data (#1, #4)** — the tax-lot polygon is the spine geometry for nearly every other dataset in this inventory (zoning, permits, affordable-housing inventory, buildable lands). A parcel-level choropleth of assessed value change, housing type, or new-unit production is achievable entirely from open data.
- **RLIS Urban Growth Boundary (#24)** over time — UGB amendment history makes a compelling animated/before-after map of regional growth, especially paired with the Buildable Lands Inventory (#25) showing residential capacity by jurisdiction.
- **Oregon Affordable Housing Inventory (#28)** — statewide, address-level, with expiring-restriction dates; can be mapped to show both current affordable stock and units at risk of losing affordability (a strong policy-relevant map).

**Best for time series:**
- **Residential Building Permits (#1)** — continuous coverage since 1995 supports a long housing-production time series, including by permit type (new construction vs. ADU vs. middle housing), and can be cross-checked against PSU's annual housing-unit estimates (#38).
- **HUD PIT/HIC national CSVs filtered to OR-501 (#32)** — supports a multi-year homelessness trend line (sheltered vs. unsheltered), though note the CoC boundary mismatch with the tri-county PSU count (#36) — OR-501 is Portland/Gresham/Multnomah County only, while PSU's count also covers Clackamas and Washington Counties' separate CoCs. Do not blend the two without reconciling geography.
- **Metro Affordable Housing Bond progress (#26)** — quarterly cadence against a fixed 2018 baseline goal makes a natural progress-bar/goal-tracker visualization.

**Best for cross-jurisdiction comparison:**
- **HUD Fair Market Rents (#34)** — directly comparable across the Portland metro, statewide Oregon counties, and nationally, using a single federal methodology.
- **PIT counts across OR-501 vs. Clackamas/Washington County CoCs (#32, #36)** — a genuine three-county comparison is possible for 2025 since all three counties coordinated a joint count that year.
- **OHCS County Housing Profiles Dashboard (#31)** — explicitly built for county-to-county comparison statewide, positioning Multnomah relative to the other 35 Oregon counties.

**Most surprising/underused:** Multnomah County's new **Homeless Services Data Dashboard (#20)**, launched April 2025, is the first time the county has published a de-duplicated monthly homelessness count (via a "by-name list") rather than only an annual point-in-time snapshot — a genuinely novel higher-frequency data source, though its short historical depth (starts 2025) limits time-series use for now.

## Notes & Caveats

**URL verification limitation (this session):** Direct fetch verification (via WebFetch and via `curl` through the sandbox's outbound proxy) was blocked at the network/policy layer for essentially all external domains tested in this environment — including unrelated control domains like `google.com` — returning `connect_rejected` / HTTP 403 from the proxy gateway itself, not from the destination servers. This affected every `.gov`, `.us`, and most other domains attempted (`portland.gov`, `multco.us`, `oregon.gov`, `gis-pdx.opendata.arcgis.com`, `gis-multco.opendata.arcgis.com`, `rlisdiscovery.oregonmetro.gov`, `hsd.multco.us`, `evictedinoregon.com`, `huduser.gov`, `pdx.edu`, `oregonmetro.gov`, etc.). As a result, **no URL in this document was confirmed via a live HTTP fetch**. All URLs were instead sourced from the WebSearch tool (which uses a separate retrieval channel) across 19 distinct search queries, and cross-referenced where the same URL appeared in multiple independent search results. Recommend a follow-up pass with working direct-fetch access to confirm HTTP 200 status on every link before publishing in the public-facing app, particularly for the deep-linked PDF and dataset-detail URLs.

**Session search-budget limit:** This session's WebSearch budget (200 calls, shared across concurrently running research subagents in this project) was exhausted before four planned confirmation searches could run (exact PortlandMaps API auth requirements, exact current Multnomah sales-ratio-study PDF link, Oregon Housing Insights Tool exact URL wording, and confirmation of the JOHS→HSD rename timeline). Where noted above as "unconfirmed," treat as needing a follow-up check.

**Pre-October 2021 permit/land-use aggregate data** is not available as open data — the City explicitly directs users to file a public records request for aggregated permit/land-use data older than October 2021 (individual property lookups on PortlandMaps remain available regardless of date).

**RLIS licensing is not uniform** — most of Metro's 200+ RLIS layers are published under an Open Database End User License (free reuse/redistribution with attribution), but some layers are instead governed by the more restrictive "RLIS Limited Use License," which does not permit redistribution. Any RLIS-derived visualization intended for public redistribution must check the license flag on each individual layer before use, not assume uniform openness across the catalog.

**Eviction data has no single authoritative public source.** The Oregon Judicial Department's full case-record system (OJCIN OnLine) is a paid/subscription service and is the only complete source of eviction filings; the public alternative, PSU's "Evicted in Oregon" project (#37), is a strong proxy but covers only ~26 of Oregon's 36 counties and only circuit-court filings (roughly 90% of all eviction cases). There is no official state or county eviction dashboard equivalent to the assessor or permit portals.

**Organizational rename in progress:** Multnomah County's "Joint Office of Homeless Services" (JOHS) has been rebranded to the "Homeless Services Department" (HSD). Both `johs.us` and `hsd.multco.us` domains surfaced in search results with overlapping content; treat `hsd.multco.us` as the current canonical domain and verify any older `johs.us`-sourced bookmark before use.

**Short-term rental bulk data availability is unconfirmed.** Portland's 2019 data-sharing agreement with Airbnb makes permitted-listing data a public record, viewable per-property on PortlandMaps, but no bulk CSV/API export was confirmed during this research pass — likely requires either a records request or site scraping for aggregate analysis.

**PHB inclusionary-housing unit-level compliance data is not published** as an open dataset (the 2024 City Auditor report found PHB itself was ~2 years behind reviewing this compliance data internally) — only aggregate counts appear in PDF reports and audits.
